# R1 Critic: Provocateur (Shklovsky)

- Anchor used: Defamiliarization (ostranenie), with its enemy, automatization: art exists to make the stone stony, to prolong perception rather than recognize.

- Observed: The core device works. Cuisine is made strange by forcing it into the Nutrition Facts form: "Cuisine Facts", "Shared*" where % Daily Value sits, "Sources: None cited yet", the home h1 "Twelve cuisines, read like the back of a box." Food is perceived as a regulated product, and the stranger "Shaped by ... Forced migration and enslavement" line sits in the same flat ledger. That friction is real. But the device is applied to form only, not to perception of the food. Content stays automatized: Mexican "Dishes Tacos, Enchiladas, Tamales, Mole"; "Savory 7 of 12" (the most shared flavor is the emptiest word). The one moment that makes a cuisine felt, Vietnamese "What sets it apart: Fresh herbs served raw", sits below the label as a footnote. Worst: the home block "Unexpected neighbors" lists "Greek and Middle Eastern 6 in common: olive oil, lemon, herbal, tangy, grilling, baking". That is the expected Mediterranean pairing, and "sweet, sour, spicy, salty" shared by Chinese and Thai are generic taste words. The heading promises a shock and the data delivers a cliche: recognition, not seeing.

- Verdict: OBJECT

- Requested change: Make the "Unexpected neighbors" block earn its name. Exclude pairs whose regions are adjacent or whose overlap is only generic flavor words (sweet, sour, salty, spicy, savory), and rank by shared concrete ingredients and techniques, so the pairs that surface are genuinely strange (Greek and Vietnamese, Indian and Korean). If none survive the filter, retitle it "Closest cross-region pairs". Also lift "What sets it apart" into the label itself as a row, so the defamiliarizing sentence is not a footnote.

- Statement of meaning: Food is a cataloged, countable product of other people's power and trade, and every count is honestly the chart's own.
