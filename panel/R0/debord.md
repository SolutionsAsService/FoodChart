# Critic: Provocateur (Debord) - R0

- **Anchor used:** Debord, The Society of the Spectacle, thesis 1 ("everything that was directly lived has moved away into a representation") and thesis 4 (the spectacle is a social relation mediated by images); plus détournement.

- **Observed:**
  - The repo holds four files and no page: "No README, no website". The name "FoodChart" promises a chart; "nothing is charted". A promise of image with no image is a pure label.
  - The data is already spectacle: whole cuisines reduced to postcard tags. Mexican = "Spicy flavors", Chinese = "Cooking techniques", French = "Famous dishes", Thai = "Popular dishes". These placeholders are the brand-slogan form of knowledge: they name the commodity and say nothing.
  - "Middle Eastern" is one entry covering many national cuisines, with no note. Italy gets "Pasta and pizza", Japan "Sushi and sashimi": the tourist-brochure canon.
  - "Regional diversity" is listed as an influence for Chinese and French: diversity is invoked as an abstraction that erases the regions.
  - "No sources are cited for any fact": no one's labour, no cook, no farmer, no archive appears. Separation is total.
  - "Minimalist Presentation" is filed as a Japanese technique: the image of the dish has displaced the act of cooking.

- **Verdict:** OBJECT

- **Requested change:**
  1. Do not build a glossy cuisine-brand gallery; that finishes the spectacle the data began. Make the chart show relations (influence flows, shared ingredients, trade routes) so the reader sees how cuisines were made by contact, conquest and trade, not as national logos.
  2. Détourn the placeholders: render "Famous dishes" and "Popular dishes" as visibly empty, flagged "unsourced / unknown" slots. Show the gaps instead of hiding them.
  3. Show a source and a confidence level on every claim, and add a visible note on "Middle Eastern" and "Regional diversity".
  4. Include the "Meiji Restoration", "Silk Road", "British" and "Mughal" entries as history (power, migration), with dated references.
  5. Give each cuisine page a "what this page leaves out" section.

- **Statement of meaning:** The artifact currently says that a cuisine is a brand, a flat list of famous names and adjectives with no maker, no source and no picture, offered as knowledge.
