# Critic: Cross-Cultural (Escobar), R0

**Anchor used:** Escobar, *Designs for the Pluriverse*: "a world where many worlds fit"; ontological design and the critique of one-world universalism, where a single schema decides what counts as real.

**Observed:**
- One schema is imposed on every cuisine: `dishes`, `ingredients`, `flavors`, `techniques`. A fixed taxonomy of Euro-American food writing becomes the only way a cuisine may appear. Whatever does not fit (ritual, season, kinship, land, who cooks) has no field.
- Flavor is reduced to a Western five-taste list: "Sweet", "Sour", "Bitter", "Spicy", "Salty", "Umami". Korean "Fermented" and Indian "Aromatic" strain against it.
- Peoples are folded into "influences" measured against Europe: "Native American (Aztec, Maya)" is a bare parenthesis and a source for Spanish; "Neighboring Countries (Laos, Cambodia, Malaysia)" is an unnamed margin; "African" in Mexican is a single word.
- Colonial history is flat, unmarked tags: "British" for Indian, "French" for Vietnamese. Nothing says conquest, trade or choice.
- Scale is erased: "Middle Eastern" is one entry for many nations, with no note. Chinese "Regional Diversity" is filed as an influence, so internal plurality becomes a placeholder.
- Hierarchy by grammar: "Traditional Japanese", "Ancient Indian", "Medieval French" place some cuisines as timeless and others as developing.
- Placeholders: "Famous dishes", "Popular dishes" outsource judgment to an unnamed global audience. No sources, no voice from any community.
- Selection: 12 cuisines are mostly Europe, East Asia and Mexico. Nothing from sub-Saharan Africa, the Andes, the Pacific or Indigenous North America.

**Verdict:** OBJECT

**Requested change:**
1. Make fields per cuisine, not universal. Allow each page to name its own terms in its own language, with the English gloss second.
2. Add `scope` (nation, region, community) and split "Middle Eastern" and "Chinese" into named regional pages, or label the lump explicitly.
3. Turn `influences` into dated, sourced relations with a stated kind (trade, conquest, migration, exchange), and give Indigenous and African contributions their own entries.
4. Cite sources; add a "who is missing" page and an open invitation for communities to speak for themselves.
5. Keep the chart, but let it show plurality (many views) rather than one ranking.

**Statement of meaning:** The artifact currently says that the world's food is a menu of twelve countries, sorted by one European-style taxonomy, in which others appear only as influences.
