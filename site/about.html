About

About the chart and its data

FoodChart is a small, open dataset of 12 cuisines and this site, which reads it. It is a starting point for comparing kitchens, not an authority on any of them.

What is in it

For each cuisine: the region, the influences that shaped it, four well-known dishes, its key ingredients, the flavors it is known for, its main techniques, and anything that sets it apart. That is 48 dishes, 45 ingredients, 15 flavors, 18 techniques and 28 influences in all.

What it leaves out

No dietary or allergen information is held. A dish listed here may or may not contain meat, dairy, gluten, nuts or anything else; check a recipe, not this chart.

Twelve entries cannot hold the world's cooking. Most of these cuisines are many cuisines: Sichuan and Cantonese cooking differ more than many neighbouring countries do, and "Middle Eastern" here stands for a family that includes Lebanese, Syrian, Palestinian, Turkish and Persian cooking. Each list is the short version a curious cook would start from.

Every count is a count of this chart

When a label says an ingredient is shared by "4 of 12", it means four of the 12 cuisines in this dataset list it. Each entry lists a handful of characteristic ingredients, not everything its cooks use, so an item listed for four cuisines may well be cooked with in many more.

Whose categories these are

The template every label follows (dishes, ingredients, flavors, techniques, influences) and the kinds of influence are this project's way of comparing kitchens, not the way these cuisines describe themselves. They were set by one author from outside them. Where a cook from one of these traditions would draw the lines differently, that is a correction worth sending.

Use the data

The whole dataset is one JSON file, free to use under the MIT license: data/cuisines.json. Each cuisine has slug, name, region, scope, summary, influences (each a name and a kind), dishes, ingredients, flavors, techniques, principles and sources.

Sources

None are cited yet. The facts here are general knowledge as written by the project, and every label says so in its Sources row. If you can point to a cookbook, a food history or a cook's own account for any line, add it to that cuisine's sources list and the label will print it.

How influences are sorted

Each influence carries a kind: the cuisine's own roots, trade and exchange, migration, empire and conquest, colonial rule, or forced migration and enslavement. "Spanish" beside "Mexican" means something different from "Silk Road trade" beside "Chinese", and the kind says how. These are judgments, not settled facts; correct them the same way as anything else.

Fix something or add a cuisine

Not on the chart yet: West African, Ethiopian and Eritrean, Caribbean, Peruvian and Andean, Brazilian, Indigenous North American, Pacific Islander, Filipino, Indonesian, Central Asian, Eastern European, Nordic. A new cuisine needs the same fields as the others, ideally from someone who cooks it.

The source is on GitHub at SolutionsAsService/FoodChart. Open an issue for a wrong fact, ideally with a source, or send a pull request that edits _src/data/cuisines.json.

Where it came from

The data began in July 2024 as two files, Cuisines.json and DetailedCusines.json. This site merged them into one: the detailed file already held every cuisine and influence in the short one, grouped ingredients such as "Spices (Turmeric, Cumin, Coriander, Cardamom)" were split so they can be compared, and entries that were in the wrong field, such as "Regional diversity" listed as an influence, moved to where they belong.
